CHAPTER 1

INTRODUCTION

1-1. SCOPE

This manual covers the operation of the SARASWATI-1 Organismic Drone Synthesis Set, hereafter called "the set". It describes the equipment, every control and indicator, the operating procedures, the call procedure for the SARASWATI ENGINE, troubleshooting and specifications. The set is intended for drones, ambient fields and outer-space signals.

1-2. EQUIPMENT DESCRIPTION

a. The set descends from the LIRA-8 project by Mike Moreno, a digital attempt at emulating the signal flow of the SOMA Laboratory LYRA-8. Its sound engine is a sample-by-sample reconstruction of that circuit, extended with the facilities described in this manual.

b. Major functional groups:

  1. Oscillator section. Ten oscillators (OSC 1–10) arranged in five blocks of two. Expandable to sixty-nine oscillators.
  2. Modulation section. Four HYPER LFOs, cross-block frequency modulation and per-oscillator modulation depth.
  3. Effects section. MOD DELAY with two lines and the TIME-1 / TIME-2 controllers, DISTORTION and REVERB.
  4. Output. VOLUME dial and a protective limiter.
  5. SARASWATI ENGINE. Call interface to the ULF relay station (Chapter 5).
  6. LIVE BACKGROUND. Optical telemetry display of the oscillators (paragraph 4-9).

c. The set requires no installation. It operates in any current web browser that supports Web Audio. Sound processing runs in a dedicated audio thread; where that facility is absent the set falls back to an equivalent main-thread processor automatically.

1-3. FRONT PANEL ARRANGEMENT

The panel is arranged in three ranks: the header (call, volume, display, information), the processor rack (modulation, delay, pitch, reverb, distortion, TIME controllers) and the oscillator bar. Index numbers in figure 1-1 are used in Chapter 3.

CHAPTER 2

FUNCTIONAL DESCRIPTION

2-1. SIGNAL PATH

a. Each oscillator produces a band-limited pulse and a triangle. The two oscillators of a block are combined in the BLOCK MIXER, where RING can turn their sum into ring modulation (paragraph 2-2).

b. The SEND MATRIX routes every oscillator individually. With T1 lit, the oscillator feeds delay line 1, whose time is TIME-1. With T2 lit, it feeds line 2 (TIME-2). An oscillator with both sends dark bypasses the delay entirely and is not affected by the delay MIX.

c. Delay output and dry signal are summed, then pass DISTORTION, VOLUME, REVERB, SOFTNESS and the limiter to the left and right outputs. When more than eight oscillators sound at once, the bus is lowered by 1/√(n/8) so the mix stays in range; up to eight oscillators the level is exactly that of the original circuit.

2-2. OSCILLATOR BLOCK

a. Oscillator core. A phase accumulator drives a band-limited (polyBLEP) pulse of 53 % duty. The triangle is obtained by integrating that pulse in a one-pole low-pass set to a quarter of the oscillator frequency. TRI ➔ SQR crossfades between the two and acts on both oscillators of the block.

b. Envelope. The key pad opens a ramp whose times are set by FAST: lit, 100 ms attack and 100 ms release; dark, 200 ms attack and an 8-second release. The ramp is squared before it reaches the amplifier. HOLD adds a standing level so that an oscillator can sound without its key. Every movement of the envelope carries a faint "thump", inherited from the original circuit.

c. Solo gate. When any S is lit, all oscillators without a lit S are muted within 3 ms. Their keys remain as they were.

d. Ring mixer. RING crossfades the block from the sum A + B to the ring product 2·A·B. A compressor follows whose threshold tracks the un-ringed level and whose ratio grows with RING. Mixing ring modulation in can therefore never make the block louder than it was before. Ring modulation multiplies: at full RING both oscillators of the block must sound.

2-3. MODULATION

a. The instantaneous frequency of each oscillator is f·(1 + MOD·SOURCE). The block SOURCE selector chooses what SOURCE is:

  1. ⇄ (labelled with the partner block, e.g. ⇄ 3·4) — the output of the partner block frequency-modulates both oscillators. Blocks 1·2 and 3·4 are partners, as are 5·6 and 7·8. Added blocks pair with their neighbour.
  2. OFF — no deliberate modulation. A trace of 0.1 % leakage remains, as in the original circuit; it keeps the drone alive.
  3. LFO — each oscillator follows the HYPER LFO chosen with its own LFO 1–4 selector.

b. HYPER LFO. Two square waves, F-A and F-B. AND multiplies them (the output flips whenever either flips), OR averages them (three-level output). LINK lets wave A push the rate of wave B by ±50 %. LFO 1 additionally drives the delay modulation when the delay SOURCE is LFO-1.

2-4. EFFECTS

a. MOD DELAY. Two delay lines. Each has a time (TIME-1, TIME-2, 1.45 ms to 5.94 s), a modulation depth (MOD-1, MOD-2, up to ±10 ms) and a shared FEEDBACK (×0 to ×4). Each line contains a compressor (−12 dB, 5:1) and a gate below −60 dB, so high feedback saturates instead of exploding. The modulation SOURCE is SELF (the line's own input, low-passed at 689 Hz — audio-rate self-modulation), OFF, or LFO-1 with WAVE TRI or SQR. MIX sets dry/wet for the oscillators that send; bypassed oscillators remain dry.

b. TIME-1 / TIME-2 controllers. They act on the TIME value of their delay line only, never on the oscillators themselves. See paragraph 4-7.

c. DISTORTION. DRIVE (+6 to +30 dB) feeds an asymmetric soft clipper whose positive peaks are pushed harder than the negative ones. MIX blends it with the clean signal.

d. REVERB. A room of eight comb filters and four all-pass filters per side, laid out after the classic ReaVerberate control set: ROOM, DAMP, WIDTH, PRE-DELAY (0–500 ms), LOW-PASS (200 Hz–20 kHz) and HIGH-PASS (20 Hz–2 kHz) on the reverberant signal, and MIX, an equal-power crossfade that keeps loudness steady from fully dry to fully wet.

NOTE

Every effect's MIX knob sits at the right-hand edge of its panel and is marked by a colour-shifting cap. On the panel, a control that "has no effect" is usually waiting for a MIX.

CHAPTER 4

OPERATING PROCEDURES

4-1. STARTING THE SET

  1. Open the set. The panel appears behind the entry screen.
  2. Press ENTER. Browsers admit sound only after a deliberate act by the operator; this is that act.
  3. Set VOLUME to about 60 before pressing any key.

4-2. ESTABLISHING A DRONE

  1. Press key pads 1 and 2. They latch and glow.
  2. Turn OSC 1 and OSC 2 while reading the frequency and note readouts. Intervals of a fifth or an octave give the classic drone; small offsets give beating.
  3. Raise TRI ➔ SQR for a brighter, reedier tone.
  4. To sustain without keys, raise HOLD and release the key pad.

4-3. CROSS-MODULATION

  1. Sound both blocks 1·2 and 3·4.
  2. Set the block SOURCE of 1·2 to ⇄ 3·4.
  3. Raise MOD on OSC 1 and OSC 2 slowly. The partner block now bends their pitch at audio rate: growl, then metal, then noise.

4-4. LFO PULSATION

  1. Set the block SOURCE to LFO.
  2. Select an LFO for each oscillator with LFO 1–4; raise MOD.
  3. Shape the rhythm with F-A, F-B, AND/OR and LINK. The LFO lamp confirms activity.

4-5. RING MIXING

  1. Sound both oscillators of the block.
  2. Raise RING. Sum and difference tones appear; the compressor holds the level (paragraph 2-2d).

4-6. DELAY SENDS

  1. Raise the delay MIX. At switch-on every oscillator sends to both lines.
  2. Darken T1 and/or T2 on oscillators that must stay dry. An oscillator with both dark is untouched by the delay.
  3. Raise FEEDBACK gradually.
CAUTION

FEEDBACK ABOVE ×1 COMBINED WITH SHORT TIMES AND HIGH DRIVE PRODUCES SELF-OSCILLATION. LOWER VOLUME FIRST.

4-7. TIME CONTROLLERS

a. RANDOM. At every interval set by SPEED, a jump of the TIME value occurs with the probability set by CHANCE. The jump lands at a random point within ±SPREAD of the TIME knob. It always glides there in 19 ms, passing through every value in between, holds for LENGTH, and glides back. A jump arriving while another is held simply replaces it.

Example: TIME-1 at 261 ms, CHANCE 78 %, LENGTH 10 ms, SPEED 50 ms, SPREAD ±28. Every 50 ms there is a 78 % chance that TIME-1 glides to a random point within 28 steps of its knob, stays 10 ms, and glides home.

b. SAW / REV. The TIME value is swept continuously, rising (SAW) or falling (REV), with a period equal to SPEED and a range of ±SPREAD. A moving delay time bends the pitch of the echoes, as on a tape machine.

c. Only oscillators sending to that line (T1 or T2 lit) are affected, and only when the delay MIX is up. The controllers never press keys or change the oscillators.

4-8. EXPANDING THE OSCILLATOR BAR

Press + at the end of the bar to add a block of two oscillators. The bar scrolls sideways. After sixty-eight oscillators the button adds a single OSC 69; then it is spent. Added oscillators repeat the tuning ranges of OSC 1–8 in order, are not affected by the PITCH knobs, and pair with their neighbouring block for cross-modulation. The × on the last added block removes it. S solos any oscillator.

4-9. LIVE BACKGROUND

With LIVE BACKGROUND on, the backdrop becomes an optical telemetry display:

  • In silence it is completely black.
  • Each oscillator is a light at its own fixed place; brightness follows its level.
  • Colour follows pitch: low tones magenta and red, through yellow, green and cyan, to violet at the top. Lights mix where they overlap.
  • An oscillator modulated by an LFO pulses with that LFO.
  • Interference: DISTORTION produces colour fringing, grain and pixel crunch; TIME jumps tear the picture; SAW/REV make it ripple; REVERB leaves trails and ghosts.

CHAPTER 5

THE SARASWATI ENGINE

5-1. GENERAL

a. The CALL SARASWATI button is not an automatic player in the ordinary sense. It is the call key of a specialized link to Station SARASWATI, a ULF transceiver server in Mongolia. Its position is withheld: 47°N 96°E SECTOR 9.

b. Ultra-low-frequency waves (300 Hz to 3 kHz) travel in the waveguide formed between the surface of the earth and the lower ionosphere. They penetrate ground and water and follow the curvature of the planet, which is why a station in the steppe can be reached from any set. The price of such a channel is a low data rate: the station does not stream sound. It listens, and it commands.

c. Once a call is accepted, the set transmits the signal of the current session — the position of every control and the state of every key — on the uplink. Commands return on the downlink and are executed on the panel exactly as an operator would execute them: knobs turn, selectors switch, keys light. The operator can watch every command arrive; controls being moved by the station are outlined in pink.

5-2. STATION ETIQUETTE

  1. Do not argue with a refusal. The station answers with silence for reasons it does not transmit.
  2. Do not end a session in the middle of a press sequence unless necessary; the pressed oscillator is released either way.
  3. The operator may play alongside the station at any time. A control the operator grasps is released by the station immediately.

MODIFICATION WORK ORDER 1

SOFTNESS CONTROL · RANDOM BUTTON

MWO 1-1. PURPOSE

SOFTNESS is the last stage of the signal path, placed beside VOLUME and ahead of the safety limiter. It removes the ear-cutting part of the sound, the harsh band between 2 and 8 kHz, and leaves the rest of the spectrum alone. It is set to 85 at switch-on. The station never moves it; only the RANDOM button does (paragraph MWO 1-4).

MWO 1-2. OPERATION

The stage is made of two parts. The first is a compressor that works only on the harsh band. The second is a gentle low-pass filter that rounds off the top of the spectrum.

MWO 1-4. RANDOM BUTTON

The RANDOM button is in the header, to the left of the call button. One press gives every control a new setting at once. It is meant for finding a starting point quickly, or for escaping a patch that has become stale.
